#### Gamma-ray Bursts as Cosmological Probes Calibrated with Strongly Lensed Quasars and Neural Networks
 - **Authors:** Wei Hong, Luca Izzo, Massimo Della Valle, Orlando Luongo, Marco Muccino, Tong-Jie Zhang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27009

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27009

 - **Abstract**
 Gamma-ray bursts (GRBs) extend distance measurements to redshifts beyond those routinely probed by Type Ia supernovae, but their use in cosmology is limited by calibration circularity and possible redshift-dependent effects. We investigate strongly lensed quasars as external calibrators of the Amati and Combo GRB relations. We reconstruct the luminosity-distance relation using an artificial neural network trained on TDCOSMO strong-lensing distances, with the absolute scale inherited from the lensing-only calibration. To avoid extrapolation, only GRBs within the lens-supported redshift interval are used for calibration. We then infer cosmological parameters through a hierarchical Bayesian analysis of flat $\Lambda$CDM, with $w_0w_a$CDM examined as a consistency test. For flat $\Lambda$CDM, Amati yields $H_0=78.26^{+17.87}_{-16.30}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_m=0.670^{+0.223}_{-0.276}$, while Combo yields $H_0=78.71^{+13.83}_{-11.29}\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$ and $\Omega_m=0.635^{+0.235}_{-0.242}$. Amati is relatively insensitive to the phenomenological redshift term, whereas Combo shows a stronger dependence. Injection--recovery tests indicate that an unmodelled positive Combo trend can bias $H_0$ and $\Omega_m$ upward. Strong-lens calibration therefore offers a route to extending the GRB Hubble diagram, but the inferred $H_0$ is not an independent GRB measurement: it retains the TDCOSMO distance scale. The precision is limited by GRB scatter, redshift-dependent systematics, and lens-modelling uncertainties.
#### Graph Neural Networks for Astrophysics and Cosmology
 - **Authors:** Christian Kragh Jespersen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27051

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27051

 - **Abstract**
 Physics seldom makes breakthroughs when we consider objects only in isolation. Instead we model and measure how objects sit among their neighbours, spatial, temporal, or otherwise: a galaxy in its environment, a halo in its history, a photon hit in a detector. Graphs are ideally suited for encoding exactly these relations, and Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) learn the geometric functions that respect them. This review presents the use of GNNs for astrophysics and cosmology, and argues that GNNs often suit physics and physical data better than any alternative: they fit the native geometry of the domain and the signal, enable the incorporation of the symmetries and prior knowledge we already trust and have toiled to build, and stay flexible enough to capture what we do not yet understand. GNNs' physically intuitive workings also render them more interpretable than other deep-learning architectures. We show how we can begin from general principles of the geometry of deep learning and build the graph and the graph neural network from the ground up, and how other architectures, like the convolutional network and the transformer, can be seen as special cases of one unified geometric blueprint. We survey applications across cosmology, galaxy formation, dynamics, and instrumentation; and we chart the frontier. From this survey, we find that only one in six articles outside instrumentation applies GNNs to data rather than just simulations. We also distil a set of best practices, including when not to reach for a graph. Throughout, we will prioritise physical intuition and foundational principles over mathematical machinery whenever possible.
#### The ALMA REBELS Survey: The [C II] Luminosity Function of UV-Selected Galaxies at $z\sim7$
 - **Authors:** Thomas Herard-Demanche, Pascal A. Oesch, Rychard Bouwens, Jacqueline A. Hodge, Hiddo S. B. Algera, Manuel Aravena, Karin Cescon, Elisabete da Cunha, Pratika Dayal, Andrea Ferrara, Rebecca Fisher, Yoshinobu Fudamoto, Kasper E. Heintz, Hanae Inami, Andrea Pallottini, Lucie E. Rowland, Renske Smit, Laura Sommovigo, Mauro Stefanon, Michael W. Topping, Paul van der Werf, Ivana F. van Leeuwen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27052

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27052

 - **Abstract**
 Luminosity functions (LF) are some of the most basic quantities providing insights into galaxy evolution across cosmic time and enabling a direct comparison between observations and simulations. Here, we present the first [CII]158$\mu$m LF at $z\sim7$ from a sizeable UV-selected ALMA sample. Our analysis makes use of 27 galaxies with significant [CII] emission line detections in the epoch of reionization from the REBELS (Reionization Era Bright Emission Line Survey) ALMA Large Program and its pilot programs. We used these line detections and similar from ALPINE to calibrate UV luminosity-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ and stellar mass-$L_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$ relations at $z\sim7$. Given the challenge in precisely defining a selection volume for the REBELS sample itself, we rely on published UV LF and stellar mass function (SMF) determinations at $z\sim7$ and three complementary approaches to derive the [CII] LF. The UV LF-based method provides a lower limit on the [CII] LF, due to its missing the most dust-obscured systems. Our SMF-based estimate yields a Schechter function with a characteristic luminosity of $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}=10^{9.11^{+0.11}_{-0.27}} L_\odot$ and a steep faint-end slope. From $z\sim7$ to $z\sim5$, our inferred [CII] LF shows an evolution of +0.3 dex in normalisation and +0.1 dex in $L^*_{\mathrm{[CII]}}$. Our results indicate a general underprediction from models of the abundance of [CII] emitters at high-redshift and thus potentially the number densities of massive yet UV-faint systems implied by our SMF-based method. Additionally, they suggest that 63% of the star formation rate density occurs in massive [CII]-bright systems and that $\sim$60-90% of [CII] emitters at $z\sim7$ may be missed by bright UV selections. This highlights the importance of surveys focusing on especially massive, UV-faint sources at $z>6$.

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27068

 - **Abstract**
 The microphysics of dark matter (DM) is imprinted on cosmological structure across an enormous dynamic range. Nonlinear cosmological observables are most reliably modeled with N-body and hydrodynamic simulations. However, systematic N-body scans over DM theory space are prohibitively expensive. Machine learning emulators provide a possible solution, but are currently limited by either low resolution or only the ability to emulate specific observables. We introduce Astrolabe, a flow-matching-based generative emulator designed to be free of both constraints. Astrolabe acts directly on the 6N phase-space coordinates of N individual particles. #### DDELenS: Tomographic Weak Lensing Simulations with Dynamical Dark Energy
 - **Authors:** Shu-Fan Chen, J. Colin Hill, Zoltán Haiman
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27088

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27088

 - **Abstract**
 We present a suite of 128 cosmological simulations in $w_0w_a$CDM cosmologies with massive neutrinos, designed to enable simulation-based inference of dynamical dark energy parameters from weak gravitational lensing and other cosmological observables. Each simulation follows $1024^3$ dark matter particles in a $512\,h^{-1}$Mpc periodic box, with cosmological parameters sampled using a Sobol sequence over the ranges $\Omega_m \in [0.28, 0.35]$, $\sigma_8 \in [0.74, 0.90]$, $w_0 \in [-1.0, -0.5]$, and $w_a \in [-1.5, 0.0]$, motivated by recent results from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument survey. The nonlinear evolution is computed with $\texttt{PKDGRAV3}$ using the N-body gauge to consistently incorporate relativistic corrections from massive neutrinos, photons, and dark energy. For each cosmology, we generate 10,000 ray-traced convergence maps at six source redshifts $z_s \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 1100\}$ using $\texttt{LensTools}$, and additionally provide halo catalogues and projected halo overdensity fields for galaxy--lensing cross-correlation analyses. We generate 100,000 realizations at the fiducial cosmology for the construction of the covariance matrix. This simulation suite provides a foundation for field-level inference and non-Gaussian statistics with next-generation weak lensing surveys such as the Rubin Observatory LSST, Euclid, and the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, as well as CMB lensing experiments including the Simons Observatory.

 - **Abstract**
 The selection of candidates belonging to rare or underrepresented stellar populations, as well as novel objects, is essential for spectroscopic follow-up. In this work, we demonstrate how combining broad- and narrow-band photometric information with machine-learning techniques enables this selection. Using photometric data in twelve filters from the Javalambre Photometric Local Universe Survey (J-PLUS) as input, we applied an anomaly detection technique based on autoencoders to identify spectral energy distributions (SEDs) that deviate from the dominant pattern in the J-PLUS objects sample. Subsequently, we applied the t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) algorithm to the J-PLUS colors of the outlier SEDs population. Combining information from the SIMBAD database and other astronomical surveys to classify the outliers identified by the autoencoder, we are able to characterize over-densities in the t-SNE map. We identified a large sample of white dwarfs and low-mass stars, including a small subset of polluted white dwarfs, and report potential candidates for spectroscopic follow-up, including 19 carbon-rich stars, 11 cataclysmic variables and 170 white-dwarfs-main-sequence binaries, all previously unreported in the literature.
#### A Radiation-Hardened, Fault-Aware Quantized Neural Network That Self-Heals
 - **Authors:** Peter Xiangyuan Ma, Joshua S. Bloom
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Machine Learning (cs.LG); High Energy Physics - Experiment (hep-ex); Nuclear Experiment (nucl-ex); Instrumentation and Detectors (physics.ins-det)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27621

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27621

 - **Abstract**
 Deep learning (DL) models are increasingly being deployed into radiation-challenging environments in spacecraft, particle accelerators, and, soon, space-based data centers. Instantiated as a learned orchestration of numbers stored in persistent memory, models are susceptible to continual degradation as high-energy particles corrupt data. At inference time, new data passed through the network fundamentally break the off-line assumptions and guarantees of a static model determined during training. To preserve the quality and efficacy of the models, fault tolerance against bit flips is a clear objective. In this work we developed a runtime method for repairing the quantized weights of neural networks (NNs) after bit flips on commodity, commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) hardware, without storing extra replicas. Our work extends the well-established Fault-Aware Training (FAT) paradigm by adding an alignment penalty to concentrate undamaged activations and weights into a low-rank subspace, and we then reconstruct flagged corrupted coordinates from the uncorrupted survivors. We call this method {\it Projection Healing} and apply it to six regression benchmarks on quantized NNs. Holding a FAT checkpoint fixed and toggling only the runtime heal gives a further ${\sim}23\%$ reduction in the native-unit excess integral. Across those datasets the method extends the operational scrub interval by a median of $2.04\times$ at a $+5\%$ RMSE degradation and $1.96\times$ at $+50\%$, compared with $1.62\times$ and $1.07\times$ for classical Single Error Correction, Double Error Detection (SECDED). This introduces a new path towards designing resilient data-driven models, complementing established hardware redundancy, ECC, and FAT-based techniques.
#### Single-Parameter Exponential Model Outperforms Standard Dark Matter and Modified Newtonian Frameworks in Predicting Galactic Rotation Curves
 - **Authors:** Mohammed Guhdar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27876

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27876

 - **Abstract**
 The functional forms of galactic rotation curves provide a fundamental test for models of gravity and dark matter. In this work, we conduct a rigorous statistical comparison of five distinct models against the complete sample of 175 galaxies from the SPARC database. The models under consideration are: (1) a one-parameter empirical law, physically constrained by each galaxy's measured flat disc rotation velocity ($V_{\text{flat}}$); (2) a classical two-parameter Freeman disk; (3) a three-parameter Navarro-Frenk-White (NFW) dark matter halo plus baryons model; (4) a one-parameter Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND) model; and (5) a two-parameter Bosma/Dark Matter Disk model. We employ a Bayesian framework with Markov Chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) for parameter estimation, and use the Akaike Information Criterion (AICc) to assess the goodness-of-fit and statistical parsimony of the best-fit models. Our results reveal a decisive preference for the constrained one-parameter empirical model. It is selected as the best descriptor for a clear majority of the sample (60.6\%) and, critically, also achieves the best median goodness-of-fit, with a reduced chi-squared of $\chi^2_{\nu} = 0.41$. In contrast, the standard NFW framework is preferred in only 12.6\% of cases, while the MOND and Freeman disk models are selected as optimal for just 10.3\% and 9.1\%, respectively. The Bosma model, despite linking dynamics to the gas distribution, is preferred in only 7.4\% of cases. The pronounced statistical and descriptive success of this simple, physically-anchored law over more complex, established theories suggests that its functional form represents a fundamental and highly efficient organizing principle in the dynamics of disk galaxies.
#### Probing Minihalos in the Late Dark Ages through Velocity Acoustic Oscillations in the 21-cm Power Spectrum
 - **Authors:** Yan Dong, Bin Yue, Chen Zhang, Yuewei Wen, Yidong Xu, Ayodeji Ibitoye, Xuelei Chen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27933

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27933

 - **Abstract**
 In the Dark Ages, minihalos above the Jeans mass accrete gas from the intergalactic medium (IGM). The accreted gas would be heated to virial temperature and produces 21 cm signal in emission, while the majority of gas retained in the IGM is cold and has 21 cm signal in absorption. The 21 cm signal of the IGM generally dominates over that of minihalos, making it difficult to extract the contribution of minihalos from the detected 21 cm signal. However, in the late Dark Ages, in regions where the gas has not yet been influenced by the radiation from Pop III stars, the 21 cm signal from the IGM is weak because collisional coupling has become less efficient. More importantly, the relic supersonic dark matter-baryon relative streaming velocities suppress the gas accretion of smaller minihalos and encode their large-scale coherent features into the 21 cm signal, generating the velocity acoustic oscillation (VAO) features on the 21 cm power spectrum. This offers a promising avenue for detecting minihalos. We found that, at $z\sim 15$ ($\sim 20$), the first peak of the net VAO wiggle at $k=0.05$ Mpc$^{-1}$ is $\sim 1.4\times 10^{-3}$ ($\sim 3.1\times 10^{-4}$) mK$^2$. With a total of 24 lunar nights/8064 hr (60 lunar nights/20160 hr) of integration, the signal from $z\sim 15$ ($\sim 20$) is detectable for an array composed of 82944 (278784) dipoles, spread over $\sim 3.5$ ($\sim 6.3$) km. If the small-scale matter power spectrum is enhanced and minihalos are much more numerous than in the standard $\Lambda$CDM model, the signal is detectable for an even smaller scale array.
#### Kinematically isolating intracluster light in AS1063: a distinct stellar population tracing the dark matter core
 - **Authors:** Callum Bellhouse, Steven Bamford, Nina Hatch, Tutku Kolcu, Johan Richard
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28013

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28013

 - **Abstract**
 We present a technique using integral-field spectroscopy to kinematically isolate intracluster light (ICL) from the brightest cluster galaxy (BCG), enabling insights into their contrasting stellar populations and the inner dark matter halo profile. Applying this technique to MUSE data of Abell S1063, we find that both components are old (~9 Gyr), but the ICL is more metal-poor than the BCG, implying distinct progenitor pathways. Within 100 kpc, the ICL comprises 55 per cent of the BCG+ICL stellar mass - substantially more than inferred from photometric decompositions. The BCG and ICL possess distinct velocity distributions, which remain nearly constant with radius, with an ICL velocity dispersion more than twice that of the BCG, and a line-of-sight velocity offset of 86 +- 18 km/s. Two-dimensional Sérsic fits yield an ICL centroid offset of 5.2 +- 1.5 kpc from the BCG. These phase-space offsets are indicative of the BCG oscillating in a broad potential minimum. Furthermore, the ICL surface-brightness profile matches that of strong-lensing estimates assuming a cored halo. Simulations indicate that the inner halo profile varies systematically for different dark matter models. Applied to larger samples, our method therefore offers a complementary route to testing the nature of dark matter, while simultaneously revealing the stellar origins of the ICL.
#### Constraints on Dark Matter Annihilation from Starlight-Induced Inverse Compton Emission in Omega Centauri
 - **Authors:** Guan-Sen Wang, Xu Pan, Lei Feng
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28068

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28068

 - **Abstract**
 The search for the particle identity of dark matter (DM) remains one of the foremost challenges in modern physics. In this work, we derive new constraints on the DM annihilation cross section $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ by considering the inverse Compton (IC) scattering of DM annihilation injected $e^+e^-$ off the starlight radiation field in Omega Centauri. As a likely remnant core of a dwarf galaxy, Omega Centauri provides a dense stellar environment that enhances the predict IC signal. Using multi-wavelength observations from Chandra and Fermi-LAT, we establish upper limits on $\langle\sigma v\rangle$ over a wide DM mass range from MeV to TeV. Notably, in the tens-of-MeV range, our results surpass existing constraints by approximately two orders of magnitude, exceeding $\langle\sigma v\rangle \sim 10^{-31}~{\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}$. At several GeV to the TeV DM masses range, our constraints also remain stronger than existing limits. Our results demonstrate that starlight induced IC emission is an important component for indirect DM searches in dense stellar systems, establishing Omega Centauri as a powerful probe of the nature of DM.
