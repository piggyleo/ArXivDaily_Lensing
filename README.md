#### Physics-Informed Neural Networks vs U-Net: Application to thermal dust emission recovery in the microwave
 - **Authors:** Nicolas Morant Juan, José M. Casas, Laura Bonavera
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31813

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31813

 - **Abstract**
 Galactic thermal dust emission is the dominant foreground contaminant of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) at frequencies above 100 GHz, and its accurate separation is essential for current and future CMB experiments. Deep learning methods, in particular convolutional architectures such as the U-Net, have demonstrated strong performance for CMB component separation, but typically operate as purely data-driven models with no explicit enforcement of the physical laws governing the emission they reconstruct. In this work, we present a Physics-Informed Neural Network (PINN) that embeds the Modified Black Body (MBB) spectral model as a hard, differentiable architectural constraint, and we compare it against a standard U-Net baseline trained under an identical pipeline. Both architectures are trained on realistic simulated Planck HFI patches at four frequencies (143, 217, 353 and 545 GHz) and evaluated on a shared validation set as well as on three independent Galactic dust realizations from the PySM phenomenological dust models named D4, D7 and D10. We find that both networks achieve comparable reconstruction quality across all evaluated datasets, with a Structural Similarity Index Measure consistently above 0.96 and a fitted angular power-spectrum spectral index that agrees with the input to within 1% in every region. Embedding the physical constraint in the PINN does not measurably degrade reconstruction accuracy relative to the unconstrained U-Net, while additionally guaranteeing that every predicted output corresponds to a thermodynamically admissible physical configuration. These results establish the physics-informed formulation as a viable methodological alternative for dust component separation, providing physical interpretability without a trade-off in reconstruction fidelity.
#### Introducing the GION Lightcone Simulation Suite for Multi-Wavelength Large-Scale Structure Cosmology
 - **Authors:** Ken Osato, Satoshi Tanaka, Tomoaki Ishiyama, Hironao Miyatake, Takahiro Nishimichi, Masato Shirasaki
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32388

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32388

 - **Abstract**
 We present the GION (Ginkaku Lightcone Multi-wavelength Simulation Project) simulation suite: 10 full-sky high-resolution cosmological lightcone simulations and their associated products for multi-wavelength large-scale structure cosmology. We use the TreePM $N$-body code GINKAKU, which is highly optimized in the massively parallel environment, and integrate the on-the-fly lightcone construction technique. Each lightcone simulation is run with $6144^3$ particles in a volume of $(1.5 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{Gpc})^3$, which corresponds to a particle mass of $1.28 \times 10^9 \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$, and extends to $z = 5$. The fine mass resolution and the depth of the lightcone simulations allow us to resolve dark matter halos down to $10^{11} \, h^{-1} \, \mathrm{M}_\odot$ in the high-redshift Universe, which is crucial for applications to the Stage-IV cosmological surveys. The fundamental statistics of the simulations, including the matter power spectrum, halo power spectrum, and halo mass function, are validated against theoretical predictions. We also generate various large-scale structure observables from the lightcone outputs: the dark matter halo catalogs, mock galaxy samples, weak lensing convergence maps, and thermal and kinematic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (tSZ and kSZ) maps. The angular power spectra of these observables, including the cross-correlation between weak lensing and the tSZ effect, are in good agreement with theoretical predictions, demonstrating that the suite provides mutually consistent multi-wavelength observables on the same lightcone. The GION simulation suite will be publicly available and can be used for various cosmological analyses to address the observational systematic effects and validate theoretical models.
#### Star clusters at cosmic dawn I: the emergence of the initial cluster mass function
 - **Authors:** Fred S. Thompson, Julien Devriendt, Adrianne Slyz, Taysun Kimm, Cheonsu Kang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32497

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32497

 - **Abstract**
 JWST observations of lensed galaxies reveal unusually bright star-cluster candidates within the first billion years after the Big Bang. However, the physical processes shaping the initial cluster mass function (ICMF) remain uncertain. We study the formation of a galaxy embedded in a dark matter halo whose virial mass reaches $10^{10} \mathrm{M}_\odot$ at $z=6$, using cosmological radiation-hydrodynamics zoom-in simulations. Its star clusters, represented by sink particles, accrete gas and form stellar populations that supply radiation and supernova feedback, thereby regulating their own growth. As we do not prescribe an efficiency per free-fall time or a fixed star-cluster formation duration, the ICMF emerges self-consistently. We assess the effects of numerical resolution and stellar radiation. Schechter fits to the central-galaxy ICMF give truncation masses near $6 \times 10^6 \mathrm{M}_\odot$, with substantial uncertainty. At fixed resolution, including stellar radiation makes the low-mass slope of the ICMF shallower, from $-1.60$ to $-1.34$, while doubling the spatial resolution steepens it back to $-1.54$. Stellar radiation also reduces the abundance of low-mass clouds, particularly those with lower gas densities, and shortens the formation time of star clusters to about a Myr, i.e. well before their first supernovae go off. On average, more rapidly fed star clusters accrete for longer, making their final masses rise faster than linearly with the initial accretion rate and contributing to a flatter ICMF. At fixed host-galaxy star formation rate, our brightest simulated clusters reach UV luminosities comparable to those of observed lensed star-cluster candidates.
#### Physics Informed Neural Network for Multi-dimensional Turbulent Self-gravitating Flows
 - **Authors:** Tirthankar De, Sayantan Auddy, Ramit Dey, Shantanu Basu
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Fluid Dynamics (physics.flu-dyn)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32840

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32840

 - **Abstract**
 Molecular clouds exhibit complex, multi-scale dynamics driven by the interplay of turbulence, self-gravity, and gas pressure. Modelling these systems with conventional grid-based methods becomes increasingly expensive as spatial resolution and dimensionality increase. Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) offer a mesh-free alternative by incorporating the governing equations directly into the training objective, but their ability to model realistic turbulent self-gravitating flows remains largely unexplored. We apply PINNs to isothermal molecular clouds initialized with astrophysically motivated turbulent velocity fields, considering subsonic and supersonic evolution in two and three dimensions. We benchmark the predicted density and velocity fields against the finite-volume code Athena++. PINNs accurately recover the global evolution of the flow over multiple free-fall times across turbulent realizations, Mach numbers, and dimensionalities. Their training cost also exhibits weak dependence on spatial resolution, increasing by less than 25% from 128^3 to 1024^3 in our experiments. These results demonstrate that PINNs can model substantially more realistic self-gravitating turbulent flows than previously explored and provide a promising complementary framework for studying these systems.
#### The Galactic Center Gamma-Ray Excess and the Look-Elsewhere Effect
 - **Authors:** Isabel S. Sands, Philip F. Hopkins
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32845

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32845

 - **Abstract**
 The origin of the excess of GeV gamma-rays in the Milky Way's galactic center observed by Fermi-LAT has been an open question for nearly two decades. The two leading interpretations of this signal are that it is produced either by annihilating dark matter (DM), or arises from unresolved point sources, such as a novel population of millisecond pulsars. In both scenarios, uncertainties and limitations in the modeling of galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission, such as the steady-state approximation of cosmic ray (CR) propagation and assumption of an axisymmetric distribution of CRs, complicate the interpretation of the galactic center excess (GCE). Simulations with full magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) and CR transport can provide a new avenue for understanding the astrophysical phenomena that drive galactic diffuse gamma-ray emission. We utilize MHD simulations of Milky Way analogs with time-dependent, spectrally-resolved CR transport to reconstruct diffuse gamma-ray emission. Comparing instantaneous emission to a time-averaged baseline, we find residuals up to several times larger than the Fermi GCE, with a wide range of spectral shapes and spatial morphologies. At some simulation snapshots, the spectrum and morphology of the residuals resemble the Fermi GCE. Our results suggest that the Fermi GCE may be a manifestation of the look-elsewhere effect- a transient fluctuation in diffuse emission from stellar sources, observed at an inopportune moment- and motivate the development of new Fermi-LAT analysis templates that account for time-dependent CR transport.
#### Complete Multi-Band Structural Characterization for 10 Million Galaxies in the Hyper Suprime-Cam Wide Survey using Deep Learning
 - **Authors:** Milind Sarkar, Aritra Ghosh, Andrew Connolly, C. Megan Urry, Andrew Coli, Joshua S. Speagle, Rhythm Shimakawa, Charles Willard, Diego Lockyer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32913

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32913

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy structure is wavelength-dependent, as different photometric bands trace distinct stellar populations and structural components. We present per-galaxy Bayesian posterior distributions of the bulge-to-total light ratio ($L_B/L_T$), effective radius ($R_e$), and flux ($F$) in the $g$, $r$, and $i$ bands for nearly $10$ million galaxies across $\sim$1400\,deg$^2$ of the Hyper Suprime-Cam Subaru Strategic Program (HSC-SSP) Wide survey at $z \leq 0.75$ and $m < 23$. To our knowledge, this is the largest multi-band catalog of bulge--disk structural parameters with per-galaxy posteriors. $\sim$3.9 million of the above galaxies have full multi-band coverage. Measurements are generated using the Galaxy Morphology Posterior Estimation Network (\gampen{}), a Bayesian deep-learning framework. We demonstrate that we can adapt \gampen{} from previously-trained single-band models to the new bands by using transfer learning on only $\sim$0.5\% of our dataset. Despite this extremely small training set, \gampen{} successfully reproduces light-profile fitting measurements and its output posteriors are extremely well-calibrated with $\lesssim6\%$ mean deviation. This approach offers a scalable pathway to structural parameter measurements with robust uncertainties for Rubin, Euclid, and Roman data, which we will pursue in forthcoming work. While structural measurements remain robust across most bands and redshift ranges, performance degrades significantly for the $g$-band at $z \gtrsim 0.5$, where it increasingly probes rest-frame ultraviolet emission; these measurements are therefore excluded from the public release. This public catalog will enable large-scale statistical studies of morphological color gradients, wavelength-dependent galaxy structure, and galaxy evolution.
#### A probabilistic approach to separate mergers and jellyfish galaxies: Application of morphological non-parametric indexes to the DESI Legacy Survey
 - **Authors:** V. M. Sampaio, Y. Jaff'e, A. Moretti, C. Lima-Dias, H. M'endez-Hern'andez, S. V'eliz Astudillo, A. Monachesi, S. Torres-Flores, A. Krabbe, C. Dulcien
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32960

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32960

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy morphology traces the physical processes that shape galaxy evolution, but non-parametric diagnostic boundaries depend on image quality and preprocessing. We investigate the classification of regular spirals, regular ellipticals, mergers, and jellyfish (ram-pressure-stripping candidate) galaxies in the DESI Legacy Imaging Surveys. Using galmex measurements of ten non-parametric morphological indices, we evaluate two-dimensional diagnostic planes with a cross-validated Bayesian kernel-density classifier and combine all indices using probability-calibrated LightGBM models. For separating regular spirals, regular ellipticals, and perturbed systems, the most informative planes combine Shannon entropy with $M_{20}$ or asymmetry indices, reaching balanced accuracies of 0.76-0.77. We provide survey-specific probabilistic boundaries in the entropy-$M_{20}$ and entropy-shape-asymmetry planes. Separating mergers from jellyfish candidates is more challenging; the best two-dimensional combinations, all involving concentration, reach balanced accuracies of approximately 0.73. The calibrated three-class LightGBM model achieves a balanced accuracy and macro-F1 score of 0.89, while the four-class model achieves 0.75 and 0.76, respectively. We provide class probabilities and preferred classifications for 1,744,454 galaxies. Our results show that non-parametric morphology identifies broad structural disturbance reliably, but distinguishing its physical origin is harder: jellyfish candidates occupy a relatively coherent region of parameter space, whereas mergers span diverse and overlapping morphologies.
