#### Dark Matter and MOND successes and degeneracies in reproducing SPARC rotation curves and weak lensing data
 - **Authors:** Amy Smith, Francesco Shankar, Daeun Jeong, Nicola Menci, Andrea Lapi, Hao Fu, Xinyu Dai, Mariangela Bernardi, Arianna Di Cintio, Annagrazia Puglisi, Alexander Belyaev, Hendrik Ulbricht, Ivette Fuentes-Guridi
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33157

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33157

 - **Abstract**
 The observed rotation velocities of spiral galaxies at large scales exceed those predicted by their baryonic components, motivating the need for dark matter (DM) or Modified Newtonian Dynamics (MOND). We fit the rotation curves of 147 late-type galaxies from the SPARC database, using a suite of models including Cold Dark Matter (CDM) models with and without baryonic feedback, Warm DM, Fuzzy DM, and MOND. We also combine SPARC kinematics with weak-lensing data to extend the mean rotation curves to ~1 Mpc. On a galaxy-by-galaxy basis, the inner SPARC rotation curves marginally favour CDM models with baryonic feedback, although accounting for the uncertainties in distances and inclinations increases the degeneracy among DM-based models and MOND. All DM models predict host halo masses larger than abundance-matching expectations, with only the CDM model with baryonic feedback reducing the scatter around the mean luminosity-halo mass relation. MOND is able to reproduce the flatness of the mean galactic rotation curves on scales up to 1 Mpc, providing stellar mass-to-light ratios within observational uncertainties. In contrast, all DM models, without the contribution of mass external to the bound halo, struggle to explain the flat rotation curves. The inclusion of the current weak-lensing data tends to favour MOND over DM models, at least at the largest scales. Future observations from BIG-SPARC, the Square Kilometre Array, and most importantly, upcoming weak-lensing surveys such as EUCLID and Rubin will provide more stringent and independent tests of these competing frameworks
#### Constraining the Inner Dark Matter Profiles of Strong-Lensing Galaxy Clusters
 - **Authors:** Emma M. Strickland, Zelie Goldberg Little, Jackson H. O'Donnell, Tesla E. Jeltema
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33370

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33370

 - **Abstract**
 We present mass modeling of three strong lensing galaxy clusters found in the Dark Energy Survey utilizing Hubble Space Telescope imaging for lens modeling, and complementary constraints from Chandra X-ray observations. All three clusters are relaxed and have giant arc or ring lens structures ideal for constraints on the dark matter distribution. The strong lens modeling and X-ray temperatures yield consistent total cluster mass estimates. We find these clusters have best-fit X-ray temperatures of $2.7 - 6.2$ keV and total masses of M$_{200} = (1-6) \times 10^{14} M_{\odot}$. Our lens models parameterize dark matter distribution with a generalized Navarro-Frenk-White (GNFW) density profile. While the GNFW profile allows the inner dark matter slope to vary, we find in all cases that the inner slopes are consistent with the standard NFW density profile $\gamma^{DM} = 1$. The X-ray surface brightness distributions for all three clusters are generally symmetric with no significant substructure, supporting the conclusion that these are relaxed clusters. In addition, we find generally low values of the external shear magnitude with median values ranging $|\gamma^{ext}| = 0.04-0.09$ and consistent with zero for two of the clusters.
#### FastPM: Fast Pretrained Model for Cosmological Simulation-Based Inference
 - **Authors:** Masaya Yamamoto, Adrian E. Bayer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33955

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33955

 - **Abstract**
 Modern cosmology increasingly relies on large ensembles of expensive simulations to model the non-linear Universe and extract information from upcoming surveys. However, generating high-fidelity simulations---whether gravity-only N-body or full hydrodynamical---requires substantial computational resources. In this work, we demonstrate that transfer learning from fast low-fidelity simulations can dramatically reduce the cost of training machine learning models, in particular for simulation-based inference (SBI). We train a Fast Pretrained Model (FastPM) on a set of Fast Particle-Mesh (FastPM) simulations to infer $\Omega_m$ and $\sigma_8$ from the power spectrum, and finetune on a smaller set of high-fidelity CAMELS N-body and hydrodynamical simulations. To reach a fixed posterior accuracy, the pretrained model requires only 5--15\% of the high-fidelity simulations compared to the number of simulations needed when training on CAMELS alone, reducing the total training simulation compute cost, including that of the pretraining simulations, by up to an order of magnitude in CPU hours. Remarkably, the gains persist for the total matter power spectrum of full hydrodynamical simulations: although FastPM contains no baryonic physics, pretraining on FastPM runs that together cost less than a single hydrodynamical simulation still reduces the total training simulation compute by a factor of $\sim$5. Moreover, pretraining on more accurate FastPM runs yields more accurate posteriors than training on CAMELS alone achieves with any number of simulations we consider. These results demonstrate that transfer learning from fast simulations is a simple and powerful route to high-fidelity SBI, and highlight the promise of simulation-based foundation models in cosmology.
#### Halo formation and distribution around cosmic web
 - **Authors:** Hongfei Yu, Huiyuan Wang, Yangyao Chen, Ziwen Zhang, Hao Li
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34042

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34042

 - **Abstract**
 We use the ELUCID simulation to study how the local environment affects the formation of dark matter halos and their spatial distribution around the cosmic web. We describe the environment by local density and cosmic-web type. At fixed web type, the halo mass function (HMF) depends strongly on the local density, whereas at fixed density it depends only weakly, though significantly, on the web type; the character of both dependencies changes around the mean density. The halo formation redshift is primarily driven by local density rather than cosmic web. We then introduce the three-dimensional cosmic-web cross-correlation function (3D-WCCF), which expresses the distribution of halos around cosmic web in the local tidal eigenframe. The 3D-WCCF provides numerous anisotropic characteristics. Around all web types, halos are most strongly correlated along the axis of weakest compression, $\mathbf{e}_3$, and least along the axis of strongest compression, $\mathbf{e}_1$. Nearly all cluster-sized halos reside in knots and lie preferentially in the plane perpendicular to ${\bf e}_1$. The 3D-WCCF also provides important information on the geometry and size of filaments, sheets and voids. Moreover, early-forming halos are more strongly correlated with the web than late-forming ones, and this assembly bias is anisotropic around filaments, sheets, and voids, being strongest along $\mathbf{e}_3$, but nearly isotropic around knots.
#### ODIN: Comparison of Source Extractor and Tractor Source Detection for Lyman Alpha Emitting Galaxy Selection at z=3.1
 - **Authors:** Bailey Thompson, Nicole Firestone, Eric Gawiser, Kyoung-Soo Lee, Vandana Ramakrishnan, Dustin Lang, Francisco Valdes, Ho Seong Hwang, Lucia Guaita, Ankit Kumar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34646

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34646

 - **Abstract**
 Lyman Alpha Emitting galaxies (LAEs) are young, actively star-forming galaxies identified by their strong Lyman-alpha emission at a rest-frame wavelength of 121.6 nm, making them valuable tracers of galaxy evolution and the large-scale structure of the Universe. The One-hundred-deg^2 DECam Imaging in narrow-bands (ODIN) survey collects LAE samples during Cosmic Noon. Because LAE selection is based on photometric measurements derived from source-detection algorithms, the choice of detection method can directly affect the resulting LAE samples. We therefore compare two prominent source detection methods, Source Extractor (SE) and Tractor (Tr), to determine which is better suited for LAE selection in ODIN. We investigate differences between SE and Tr in the ODIN-COSMOS field, focusing on the z=3.1 LAE sample. We find that a significant fraction of Tr LAEs have low continuum flux measurements, increasing the inferred narrow-band excess and resulting in an LAE sample nearly twice as large as SE. However, LAEs detected by Tr but not SE exhibit equivalent width estimates indicative of potential contamination. We introduce an additional LAE selection criterion based on the expected Lyman-break behavior of LAEs at z~3 to reduce low-redshift contamination without requiring a significant detection of the break itself. Our results demonstrate that differences in source detection can significantly affect narrow-band LAE selection and motivate the use of SE photometry for ODIN LAE catalogs.
#### Machine Learning Applications in the Solar Interior and Atmosphere
 - **Authors:** Vishal Upendran, Carlos J. Díaz Baso
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); Machine Learning (cs.LG)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34687

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34687

 - **Abstract**
 The solar interior and atmosphere constitute a strongly coupled, multiscale dynamical system defined by continuous temporal evolution. Predicting the state of this system requires modeling physical interactions that span disparate spatial and temporal scales, constrained by heterogeneous and sparse data. This chapter formalizes the Sun within the mathematical framework of dynamical systems theory, defining a global state vector ($\vec{S}$) governed by thermodynamical, kinetic, and magnetic properties. Because solar observations represent projected, multi-modal, and partially observable measurements ($\vec{O}[\vec{S}]$) of this hidden state, pure physics-based deduction is fundamentally constrained. We present a unifying thesis: the primary role of machine learning in heliophysics is to act as a structured family of mathematical operators---specifically Inference ($\mathcal{I}$), Emulation ($\mathcal{E}$), Propagation ($\mathcal{P}$), and Control ($\mathcal{C}$)---that reconstruct, simulate, and couple this global state vector across disparate physical layers. By framing the vast, multi-disciplinary literature within this unified state-space taxonomy, we demonstrate how data-driven techniques bridge observational gaps to resolve hidden multiscale couplings and dramatically accelerate the simulation of non-linear processes, establishing a rigorous pathway to predict the global evolution of the Sun.
#### Data-driven acquisition offset correction for telescope networks using machine learning and sensitivity analysis
 - **Authors:** Jackson Zariski, Kaitlin Kratter, Mark Bowman, Nandini Dharwadkar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34719

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34719

 - **Abstract**
 Reliable target acquisition is essential for maximizing observing efficiency, particularly for robotic telescope networks that operate across many sites, instruments, and environmental conditions. Although these facilities generate large volumes of operational and observational data, acquisition procedures often continue to rely on static pointing models and manual adjustments by observatory staff. These approaches can be effective, but they may not fully capture changing behavior associated with telescope configuration, observing conditions, instrument state, or other time-dependent effects. We present a data-driven machine-learning pipeline for automatically predicting and correcting telescope acquisition offsets, demonstrated using archival operational logs from the Las Cumbres Observatory network. Our approach applies both traditional machine-learning methods and deep-learning regression models to learn relationships between measured acquisition offsets and a range of observing, telescope, and instrumentation conditions. By using historical performance data, the pipeline is designed to complement existing pointing models and reduce the need for repeated manual correction. We also present an open-source, Dockerized software service that makes the trained models accessible through a browser-based graphical interface. The service is intended to support straightforward, real-time use by observatory staff while remaining portable across computing environments. Together, the prediction pipeline and deployment framework provide a practical path toward more adaptive, automated, and data-informed telescope acquisition.

#### Cracking into RACS - I. Measuring the cosmic dipole in the RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 radio surveys with simulation-based inference
 - **Authors:** Oliver T. Oayda, Mali Land-Strykowski, Stefan W. Duchesne, Emil Lenc, Tara Murphy, Geraint F. Lewis
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35207

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35207

 - **Abstract**
 We measure the dipole in the count of radio galaxies over the sky as seen by the Australian SKA Pathfinder (ASKAP) in the Rapid ASKAP Continuum Survey (RACS). Our analysis focuses on the third observational epoch in the 'low' passband (943.5 MHz; RACS-low3) and the first epoch in the 'mid' passband (1367.5 MHz; RACS-mid1). We find strong evidence for a systematic relation between flux density and the temperature at the time of observation, whereby reported flux densities are suppressed to a greater degree when the ASKAP antennas are warmer. We include this temperature response in our forward model as part of our Simulation-based Inference (SBI) pipeline, generating data realisations that are then used by a neural network to learn the likelihood function. Despite this model, RACS-low3 and RACS-mid1 both exhibit a dipole inconsistent with the kinematic dipole of the Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB), adding further evidence for the 'cosmic dipole tension'. For RACS-low3, the dipole agrees in direction but has an amplitude four times in excess, while the dipole for RACS-mid1 is offset from the CMB and has an amplitude roughly five times in excess. Our findings underscore the importance of interrogating the data for systematic effects and showcase the power of SBI in handling them.
#### Inferring the Total Mass of Galaxy Halos Using Photometry
 - **Authors:** Chris Brook, Arianna di Cintio, Claudio Dalla Vecchia, Salvador Cardona Barrero, Elena Arjona-Gálvez
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35366

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35366

 - **Abstract**
 Galaxy size, as defined as the radius at which the stellar surface density reaches $1~\mathrm{M}_\odot\,\mathrm{pc}^{-2}$ ($R_1$), has been shown to correlate more tightly with stellar mass than the effective radius. Cosmological simulations further predict a tight relation between $R_1$ and dark matter halo mass ($M_{200}$), but this prediction has not yet been tested observationally. We present the first observational test of the predicted $R_1$--$M_{200}$ relation and assess whether galaxy size, defined through $R_1$, can serve as a robust estimator of dark matter halo mass. We analyze galaxies from the SPARC database, combining high-quality rotation curves to infer dark matter halo masses with stellar surface density profiles derived from photometric data to measure $R_1$. We then compare the observed relation with the predictions from state-of-the-art cosmological simulations. We find good agreement between observations and simulations: galaxies follow the same tight $R_1$--$M_{200}$ relation predicted by numerical models across the explored mass range, with low intrinsic scatter. We provide the first observational validation of the $R_1$--$M_{200}$ relation, demonstrating that $R_1$ is a simple, physically motivated, and robust estimator of dark matter halo mass. Because $R_1$ is determined entirely from photometric observations, it provides a tool for inferring halo masses from imaging data alone, with applications to studies of galaxy formation and evolution.

#### Classification of Fermi Blazars of Uncertain Type Using Positive-Unlabelled Machine Learning
 - **Authors:** Sikandar Akbar, Zahir Shah, Zahoor Malik, Athar A. Dar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35573

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35573

 - **Abstract**
 We apply positive-unlabelled (PU) machine learning to the 1623 blazars of uncertain type (BCUs) in the Fermi Large Area Telescope Fourth Source Catalogue, Data Release 4 (4FGL-DR4). Five PU classifiers are trained on seven gamma-ray features, once with the 820 flat-spectrum radio quasars (FSRQs) and once with the 1490 BL Lac objects as the labelled class (FSRQ mode and BL Lac mode). Each source is scored out of fold over 100 repeated five-fold splits, and its confidence is the fraction of splits in which at least three of the five classifiers flag it. At a confidence of at least 0.1, 437 BCUs are FSRQ-like, 597 are BL Lac-like, 12 are flagged in both modes and 577 in neither. On known sources the ensemble reaches a recall of 0.80 in FSRQ mode and 0.82 in BL Lac mode at a false-positive rate of 0.08, but a logistic regression trained on the labelled sources alone does at least as well, so PU learning gives no accuracy gain here. Removing the sky coordinates from the features does not lower the performance on known sources but adds 107 BCUs to the candidate lists and drops 30, which is consistent with the coordinates acting as a selection shortcut, so we exclude them. We check the candidates against published optical spectra, SDSS-V spectra, and BCUs that later received a class in the catalogue. Among sources assigned a single class, BL Lac-like calls are correct for 84 to 100% of the sources and FSRQ-like calls for 58 to 83%, depending on the sample. These samples come from spectroscopic follow-up and do not represent all BCUs. The candidates are fainter and lie closer to the Galactic plane than the labelled sources, and the excess of unclassified BCUs in the plane is not explained. We release the confidences of all 1623 BCUs.
