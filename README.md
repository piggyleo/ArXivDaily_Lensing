 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14781

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14781

 - **Abstract**
 We present cosmological constraints from 11 strongly lensed quasars with measured time delays (hereafter, the TDCOSMO-2026 sample). In addition to expanding the sample, we improve many aspects of our blind analysis with respect to our 2025 analysis. Among other improvements, spatially resolved kinematics are available for nine systems, including six with JWST-NIRSpec, compared to one in 2025; for three lens models, JWST-NIRCam images now replace HST; axisymmetric dynamical modeling is applied directly to the spatially resolved data, instead of an axisymmetric correction factor applied to spherical models; and the line-of-sight convergence is now based on the Euclid Flagship simulations instead of the Millennium Simulation. Our baseline and primary result is based on the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, in combination with a prior on $\Omega_{\rm m}$ from the DES-Dovekie supernova sample. In the flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter ($\Lambda$CDM) cosmology, we find $H_0=71.1^{+2.4}_{-2.3}$ km.s$^{-1}$.Mpc$^{-1}$, a 3.3% uncertainty that accounts for the mass-sheet degeneracy. The SLACS and SL2S samples are in excellent agreement with the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, improving the precision on $H_0$ in flat $\Lambda$CDM to 3.0%. Using the DESI-DR2 baryon acoustic oscillations (BAO) likelihood instead of DES-Dovekie yields fully consistent results. We also present constraints in the open $\Lambda$CDM, $w$CDM, $w_0w_a$CDM, and $w_{\phi}$CDM cosmologies; the TDCOSMO $H_0$ inference is robust and consistent across all of them. The combination of the TDCOSMO+SLACS+SL2S likelihood with those from DESI-DR2 and DES-Dovekie disfavors $w=-1$ at the $2.3$-$2.5\sigma$ level in all three dark energy extensions that we tested. We find, e.g., $w_0=-0.88^{+0.04}_{-0.05}$ in $w_{\phi}$CDM and $w_0 = -0.85^{+0.07}_{-0.06}$ in $w_0w_a$CDM, while $w_a = -0.48^{+0.46}_{-0.44}$ remains consistent with zero.
#### TDCOSMO 2026: new insights on the Hubble tension from strong lensing time delays
 - **Authors:** TDCOSMO Collaboration, Simon Birrer, Michele Cappellari, Frédéric Courbin, Christopher D. Fassnacht, Pierre Fleury, Joshua A. Frieman, Aymeric Galan, Xiang-Yu Huang, Daniel P. Johnson, Sanjana Khanolkar, Shawn Knabel, Hrvoje Krizic, Martin Millon, Takahiro Morishita, Veronica Motta, Pritom Mozumdar, Eric Paic, Hadrien Paugnat, Allan G. Schweinfurth, Anowar J. Shajib, William Sheu, Dominique Sluse, Massimo Stiavelli, Sherry H. Suyu, Tommaso Treu, Felipe Urcelay, Devon M. Williams, Kenneth C. Wong
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14881

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14881

 - **Abstract**
 We discuss the measurement of the Hubble constant ($H_0$) in flat $\Lambda$ cold dark matter cosmology based on the blind hierarchical analysis of 11 multiply-imaged quasars (hereafter the TDCOSMO-2026 sample) and described in greater detail in a companion paper. In addition to the time delays, we use lens models based on high-resolution imaging from the Hubble Space Telescope (HST) and from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST), analyzed jointly with axisymmetric dynamical models based on JWST-NIRSpec, VLT-MUSE, and Keck-KCWI spectroscopy. Our primary result $H_0=71.1^{+2.4}_{-2.3}$ km.s$^{-1}$.Mpc$^{-1}$ is based on the TDCOSMO-2026 sample, in combination with $\Omega_{\rm m}$ constraints from the DES-Dovekie supernova (SN) dataset. Our 3% precision accounts for the mass-sheet degeneracy. Importantly, our measurement is independent of the local distance ladder (LDL). Among individual late-universe probes, only the traditional LDL is more precise. We discuss our result in the context of the Hubble tension, the difference between $H_0$ measured in the late universe and the prediction based on early-universe probes such as the cosmic microwave background (CMB). We construct combinations of independent late-universe probes and show that the tension with the Planck CMB prediction is robust to excluding any one of the methods based on Type-Ia Supernovae, Cepheids, or the Tip of the Red Giant Branch. For example, we construct a 'no-SN' $H_0$ measurement, by replacing the DES-Dovekie constraint on $\Omega_{\rm m}$ with that from DESI baryon acoustic oscillations, and then combining TDCOSMO-2026 with independent non-SN late-universe probes. This 'no-SN' late-universe probe is in 3.7$\sigma$ tension with Planck. Even after completely excluding the LDL, the tension with Planck persists at the 2.7$\sigma$ level.
#### The NIVARIA-Local Group simulations: Unveiling the origin of rotation curve diversity in dwarf galaxies and its connection to galaxy morphology
 - **Authors:** A. Contreras-Santos, A. Di Cintio, A. Negri, J. Sarrato-Alós, S. Cardona-Barrero, G. García-Bethencourt, C. Brook, N. I. Libeskind, A. Knebe, A. Macciò
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15071

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15071

 - **Abstract**
 Dwarf galaxies exhibit a large diversity in rotation curve shapes, challenging galaxy formation models within the $\Lambda$CDM paradigm. We present NIVARIA-LG, a new suite of cosmological simulations studying dwarf galaxy evolution in realistic Local Group environments. This intermediate-resolution pilot lays the groundwork for the full-scale NIVARIA project. The simulations combine constrained Local Group initial conditions from HESTIA with the NIHAO galaxy formation model. The simulated galaxies reproduce observed stellar-to-halo mass relations, match the Baryonic Tully-Fisher relation, and capture the distribution of stellar effective radii, including compact and diffuse galaxies. We analyse the structural and kinematic properties of isolated dwarf galaxies with $10^7 \lesssim M_\star/M_\odot \lesssim 10^{10}$, focusing on rotation curve diversity. NIVARIA-LG naturally produces a broad range of rotation curve shapes, including slowly rising systems underrepresented in previous simulations and steep rotation curves. We find that rotation curve diversity is primarily associated with gas extent and inner dark matter structure: more extended gas and shallower inner density profiles correspond to more slowly rising curves, while stellar effective radius shows little independent association. Individual galaxies show that rotation curve shapes change rapidly as gas is redistributed during assembly. Recent accretion events are also associated with more slowly rising curves in the most massive dwarfs. Together, these results suggest that rotation curve diversity is linked to the baryonic cycle, which shapes the inner dark matter distribution. The Local Group environment and its filaments influence this evolution by regulating gas accretion and redistribution, producing the observed diversity in rotation curve shapes.
#### Equation of State Classification of Core-Collapse Supernovae with a Network of Gravitational-Wave Detectors
 - **Authors:** A. Akhmetali, Y. S. Abylkairov, J. A. Font, M. Zanolin, E. Abdikamalov
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16130

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16130

 - **Abstract**
 Gravitational waves from core-collapse supernovae may provide a unique probe of the equation of state (EOS) of dense nuclear matter. In previous work, we assessed the potential of machine-learning methods for EOS classification for rapidly rotating supernovae using individual gravitational-wave detectors. Here, we extend the framework to detector networks and investigate the impact of detector configuration, source distance, arrival time, and sky location on the classification. Using convolutional neural networks, we perform 10-class EOS classification directly from noisy detector data for both second- and third-generation observatories. We find that detector geometry strongly influences classification performance. For Galactic events, current detector networks can achieve promising classification performance, but the reach depends sensitively on the detector network antenna response. Adding detectors improves time-averaged performance and produces more uniform classification across the sky. Third-generation observatories extend the classification reach to 100 kpc for favorable source orientations but do not remove the dependence on detector geometry.

#### Self-Interacting Dark Matter: From Particle Physics Theories to Structure Formation Simulations
 - **Authors:** Martin Rosenlyst, Vinh Tran, Oliver Zier, Xuejian Shen, Mark Vogelsberger, Thejs Brinckmann, Mads T. Frandsen, Jesús Zavala, Frederik Holt Dihver Christensen, Mikkel Theiss Kristensen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16968

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.16968

 - **Abstract**
 We develop a unified theory-to-simulation framework for self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), connecting microscopic dark-sector physics to nonlinear structure formation. We systematize particle-physics ingredients of representative SIDM models and map microscopic parameters and interactions onto structure-formation simulation inputs. These include internal-state populations and reaction networks, velocity- and angle-dependent cross sections, inelastic and dissipative energy exchange, decay rates and recoil kinematics, and modifications to cosmological initial conditions. The numerical framework builds on the efficient, scalable SIDM infrastructure introduced in our companion work for the moving-mesh code AREPO-2, providing substantial performance gains over AREPO-1. We implement isotropic and anisotropic self-scattering with constant or velocity-dependent cross sections, multi-state and inelastic reactions, and dissipative scattering, complemented by a dedicated dark-matter decay module. For strongly forward-peaked interactions, the anisotropic hybrid SIDM scheme combines frequent small-angle drag-diffusion with explicit rare large-angle scattering, achieving speed-ups of up to $\sim100\times$ relative to fully anisotropic Monte Carlo in the tested configurations. We validate the implementations through beam-lattice deflection experiments, thermalization and uniform-box decay tests, isolated-halo gravothermal core-collapse simulations, and cosmological box and zoom-in simulations. These tests recover analytical predictions, establish consistency between equivalent numerical treatments, and reproduce published inelastic and dissipative SIDM benchmarks. The resulting modular framework connects dark-sector models to nonlinear astrophysical predictions, enabling exploration of microscopic interactions beyond standard isotropic, elastic SIDM.
#### Indirect Dark Matter Detection in the Multi-Messenger Era: Experiments, Systematics, Anomalies, and Prospects for Discovery
 - **Authors:** Muad Ghaith, Khaled Obaideen
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17013

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17013

 - **Abstract**
 Indirect searches for dark matter (DM) span gamma rays, charged cosmic rays, neutrinos, X-rays, radio emission, the cosmic microwave background (CMB), and the 21~cm signal, but reported constraints and anomalies vary in evidential strength. This critical review synthesizes peer-reviewed literature from January 2000 through October 2026 using a structured search strategy. We assess observations through a framework linking particle interactions and halo source terms to propagation or cosmological energy deposition, instrumental response, background modeling, and statistical inference. Fermi-LAT observations of dwarf spheroidal galaxies provide robust GeV-scale benchmarks for DM annihilation, whereas Galactic-center searches offer higher expected signals but depend more on foregrounds and halo profiles. Charged-cosmic-ray measurements are precise but limited by propagation and source uncertainties, while neutrino searches offer complementary sensitivity through solar capture, directional and flavor information, and high-mass annihilation or decay. Several widely discussed signals, including the 3.5~keV line, 511~keV emission, the Galactic-center GeV excess, charged-particle features, cluster radio excesses, and candidate gamma-ray lines remain inconclusive because of limited independent confirmation, uncertain target scaling, spectral degeneracies, or background-modeling limitations. Cosmological observations provide complementary constraints from CMB energy-injection limits and 21~cm measurements of the intergalactic medium. Artificial intelligence increasingly supports reconstruction and inference but requires rigorous calibration and domain-shift validation. The strongest case for DM discovery would require statistically consistent evidence across independent targets, instruments, or messengers, under common particle-physics parameters, and with well-characterized uncertainties.

 - **Abstract**
 The decay of dark matter (DM) is a generic prediction of many particle physics models, producing distinctive monochromatic signatures in environments such as the Galactic halo. Searches for decaying DM in the elusive 'MeV gap' have remained conspicuously under-explored, despite well-motivated models including axion-like particles, sterile neutrinos, and hidden-sector scenarios predicting signatures in this energy range. The upcoming Compton Spectrometer and Imager (COSI) telescope will help bridge this gap. #### Untangling Similarities and Differences in Star Formation Histories Across Cosmological Simulations
 - **Authors:** Alicia M. Savelli, Matthew A. Kustec, Joshua S. Speagle, Kartheik G. Iyer, Eshal Ahmed, Zehao Peng, Norman Murray, Cissy Kuang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17359

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17359

 - **Abstract**
 How galaxies assemble depends on processes ranging from small-scale baryonic physics to large-scale cosmological assembly, which are difficult to disentangle with observations alone. Using $\sim$63,000 simulated galaxies from seven cosmological simulations spanning hydrodynamical, semi-analytic, and semi-empirical models, we explore how specific star formation histories (sSFHs) vary across modelling assumptions. Using non-linear dimensionality reduction (UMAP), we find that sSFH shape variation organizes along a few smooth axes in a 2D embedding, broadly corresponding to (1) peak timing, (2) peak sharpness, and (3) subsequent quenching rate. We identify sSFH shapes that many simulations share ("common"), well described to first order by dark matter halo assembly, and a few tied to individual simulations ("unique"), which likely reflect subgrid and parametrization choices such as merger-triggered burst prescriptions. A notable exception is a common population of rapidly quenched galaxies found across most simulations, likely driven by e.g., active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback, that halo assembly alone cannot reproduce. The origin of the most distinctive unique shape, an Illustris-dominated history with an early peak and slow decline, remains unclear, but we test the effects of short-term variability. With a multi-objective auto-encoder neural network, we recover the simulation that a given sSFH is drawn from with $\sim$95\% out-of-sample accuracy; ablation tests indicate this information is embedded in long-timescale sSFH morphology rather than short-term variability. These results provide avenues for theoretical and observational follow-up connecting star formation histories to subgrid physical models.

 - **Abstract**
 Weak gravitational lensing offers a direct probe of the projected dark matter distribution in cluster-scale structures and the surrounding cosmic web. We investigate the capability of the China Space Station Telescope (CSST) to recover non-spherical dark matter morphologies using mock reduced-shear maps generated from cosmological N-body simulations. We apply the deep-learning-based MIU2Net mass-reconstruction framework to CSST-like wide- and deep-field source densities, focusing on two representative systems: filament-connected halo pairs and elongated halos. MIU2Net accurately recovers halo centers, elongated mass distributions, and the filamentary structures connecting halo pairs in both survey configurations. Halo centers are localized within about 0.1 h^(-1) Mpc, and elongated-halo shapes and orientations are reliably recovered. Filament paths are stable against shape noise, with mean normalized instability parameters I_F, defined as the mean deviation from the true filament locus in units of R_200, decreasing from 0.041 to 0.037 in the low-redshift bin and from 0.051 to 0.045 in the high-redshift bin from the wide to deep field. The recovered filament shapes remain consistent with the true maps at the percent level. Compared with conventional Kaiser-Squires reconstruction, MIU2Net better preserves low-contrast anisotropic structures while suppressing small-scale noise. These results demonstrate that CSST weak-lensing data, combined with machine-learning-based mass reconstruction, will enable statistical studies of cluster assembly, anisotropic matter accretion, and the connection between massive halos and the filamentary cosmic web.

 (Abridged) Baryonic feedback is expected to transform cuspy dark-matter (DM) haloes into cores, but direct observational links between inner DM slopes and galaxy star-formation histories (SFHs) remain limited. We investigate and quantify the connection between halo inner slopes and baryonic assembly histories in a large sample of intermediate-z galaxies. We analyse 375 galaxies at 0.1<z<1.43 spanning 7<log(Mstar/Msun)<11.2 using 3D forward modelling of MUSE kinematics and disk-halo decompositions with mass-dependent (DC14-based) and generalised Navarro-Frenk-White (gNFW) halo profiles. Bayesian model comparison shows that the DC14-based halo density profiles are statistically preferred over gNFW for a substantial fraction of the sample. Partial correlation coefficient and random forest regression analyses consistently identify stellar mass (or, equivalently, the stellar-to-halo mass ratio) as the primary driver of the inner density slope, a result that holds robustly even when the analysis is restricted to the gNFW fits. Cored systems preferentially occupy lower-mass, more bursty bins with later assembly times, whereas cuspy systems are associated with higher stellar masses, lower star formation activity, and earlier assembly epochs, in agreement with theoretical expectations of stellar-feedback-induced cores.

 Accurate forecasting of optical turbulence (OT) is useful to optimise telescope scheduling and maximising observing efficiency, as the performance of adaptive optics systems strongly depends on turbulence conditions. While various numerical weather prediction and machine learning models are developed to predict these complex turbulence properties, properly evaluating their added value requires well-defined baseline reference models. This study establishes baseline performance thresholds for key astroclimatic parameters using two distinct models: one forecasting the parameter average over a specific timescale, and the other forecasting the temporal evolution over the same specific timescale. #### Evidence for Distinct One-Off and Repeating Subpopulations in CHIME/FRB Catalog 2
 - **Authors:** Sloane Sirota, Duncan R. Lorimer
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18710

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18710

 - **Abstract**
 The Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment (CHIME) has published its second catalog of 3,641 fast radio bursts (FRBs). While this sample includes 981 bursts from 83 repeating sources, only 2.3% of CHIME FRBs are observed to repeat. This raises the question of whether the FRB population truly consists of a single repeating population or includes a distinct subpopulation of one-off sources. We apply Simulation Based Inference (SBI), a likelihood-free machine learning framework, to model the CHIME/FRB Catalog 2 dataset, testing eight FRB population models, half of which include a subpopulation of truly one-off sources. The current catalog strongly and decisively favors models including such a subpopulation, with the best-fitting model combining a log-normal burst rate distribution, a Poissonian wait-time distribution, and a one-off subpopulation. This preference is corroborated by an independent random forest classifier and by the ability of one-off models to naturally reproduce the hyperactive repeating sources present in the catalog without having been trained on them. We place upper limits on the potentially observable CHIME/FRB population of 36,000 repeating sources and 19 one-off sources per day in the CHIME sky. Models with a one-off subpopulation naturally account for the hyperactive repeating sources present in the catalog without having been trained on them, providing an independent consistency check on our results. We predict the repeater percentage will fall below 2% in the next five years, eventually declining to ~0.5% as the sample saturates with one-off detections.
