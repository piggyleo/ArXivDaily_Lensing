#### TNGC-SIDM I: Evolution of massive cluster mergers under the self-interacting dark matter paradigm
 - **Authors:** Wonki Lee, Dylan Nelson, Myungkook J. Jee, Giulia Despali, Mark Vogelsberger, Annalisa Pillepich, Cian Roche, Kyle Finner, Hyungjin Joo, Daisuke Nagai, John ZuHone
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18957

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18957

 - **Abstract**
 Merging galaxy clusters provide an ideal laboratory for testing the self-interacting nature of dark matter. In this paper, we present a pilot study of magnetohydrodynamical cosmological zoom-in simulations of two cluster mergers selected from TNG-Cluster, with $M_{\rm 200c} \sim 9\times10^{14}~M_\odot$ and $M_{\rm 200c}\sim 2\times10^{14}~M_\odot$. The simulations are performed with both standard collisionless cold dark matter (CDM) and self-interacting dark matter (SIDM), assuming an isotropic, velocity-independent cross-section of $\sigma_{\rm T}/m_\chi = 1~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$, with an additional $0.5~\rm cm^2~g^{-1}$ run for the low-mass system, and the mass resolution and galaxy formation model are unchanged from TNG-Cluster. We show how dark matter self-interactions produce an isothermal core in the dark matter halo, generate unique substructures in the intracluster light, and introduce variation in the multi-wavelength observables of the intracluster plasma. Using these simulations, we test the validity of observational probes for dark matter self-interactions in cluster mergers. We find that the shock-to-shock separation shows similar evolution in the self-interaction models tested here, providing a reference for distinguishing the $\sim100-200~\rm kpc$-scale difference in halo separation induced by self-interactions. Moreover, the dark matter halo centroid of the main cluster in the massive cluster merger is offset from the brightest cluster galaxy by as much as $\sim20~\rm kpc$, larger than the offset in the sub-cluster. This offset persists beyond 4 Gyr after the first pericenter passage. Our results indicate that sample selection plays a key role in testing the possible self-interacting nature of dark matter using cluster mergers, and that double radio relic cluster mergers and massive clusters with evidence of an ancient major merger offer a promising testbed for this purpose.
#### Wave Cold Dark Matter Can Reproduce the Radial Acceleration Relation in X-ray Elliptical Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Runyu Meng, Xiaobo Dong
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18969

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18969

 - **Abstract**
 Wave cold dark matter (wave CDM) is a well-motivated variant within the collisionless CDM paradigm, in which the de~Broglie wavelengths of ultralight bosons become relevant on galactic scales. While many of the previous tests of wave CDM have focused on small-scale tensions involving solitonic cores and certain soliton--halo relations in galaxies, the radial acceleration relation (RAR) provides a complementary test: for a given baryonic mass distribution, it probes whether the DM halo can reproduce the observed total gravitational field. In this work we test wave CDM against the RAR using eight X-ray elliptical galaxies from the compilation of Lelli et al. We perform a suite of 3D Schrödinger--Poisson simulations and evolve the wave-CDM halos to virialized quasi-stationary states. For each galaxy and each adopted boson mass, we select the realization whose final snapshot most closely matches the empirical RAR. We find that the selected wave-CDM realizations for these elliptical galaxies can reproduce the RAR without including baryonic feedback or dark-matter self-interactions. At the boson mass $m_a=10^{-23}$eV, the combined fit to the eight galaxies gives $a_0=1.19\times10^{-10}\,\mathrm{m\,s^{-2}}$, consistent with the canonical value inferred from disk galaxies. When the boson mass is varied over $3\times 10^{-24}$ eV $\le m_a \le 2\times 10^{-23}$ eV, the best-fit value of $a_0$ changes by only $\sim 11\%$, and this variation shows no systematic trend with $m_a$. This insensitivity to boson mass arises because, over the radial range relevant for the RAR, the virialized halos are supported mainly by their velocity field, whereas the boson-mass-dependent quantum-pressure term is dynamically subdominant. These results show that wave CDM can reproduce the RAR in elliptical galaxies, and provide a new galactic-scale test of the model beyond the previous focus on solitonic cores.
#### GroArnold: A Framework for Constructing Action-based Models for Triaxial Galaxies and Dark Matter Halos
 - **Authors:** Jian-Yu Gu, Xiao-Bo Dong, Shi-Yan Zhong, Run-Yu Meng
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18978

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18978

 - **Abstract**
 Action-based distribution functions $f(\mathbf{J})$ provide a powerful description of stellar systems. Consequently, the construction of physically accurate and dynamically stable $f(\mathbf{J})$ for galactic components has emerged as a key challenge in the field. In this study, we present GroArnold, a computational framework designed to construct analytical $f(\mathbf{J})$ expressions for axisymmetric and triaxial galaxies and dark matter halos. The framework integrates modules for $N$-body initial condition generation, $N$-body simulation, angle--action variable estimation, and distribution-function fitting into a unified workflow. It supports a wide range of density profiles and kinematic properties, and is uniquely designed to generate stable $N$-body models for user-specified galactic systems, with the aim of obtaining an $f(\mathbf{J})$ that approximates a dynamically stable and physically consistent state. To this end, we extend the triaxial Stäckel Fudge method of the TACT code, implemented in our Nbody-TACT module, to enable action estimation directly from $N$-body gravitational potentials. GroArnold provides a robust and flexible tool for deriving numerically empirical, explicitly analytical distribution functions for user-specified galaxies or halos, thereby facilitating a wide range of practical $f(\mathbf{J})$-based modeling applications. In addition, it serves as a powerful tool for investigating the dynamics of realistic triaxial galaxies and halos beyond the scope of traditional spherical or axisymmetric models. We assessed the accuracy of the output $f(\mathbf{J})$ by comparing its predicted spatial and velocity distributions with those of the input $N$-body snapshot, and confirmed that it provides a reasonably accurate representation of the input. The code is modular, publicly available, and designed to support future extensions and adaptations.
#### Kinetic Theory of the EFTofLSS : A Halo-Based Approach
 - **Authors:** Om Kishor Hebbar, Caio B. de S. Nascimento, Arka Banerjee
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19001

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19001

 - **Abstract**
 The Effective Field Theory of Large Scale Structure (EFTofLSS) provides a coarse-grained description of the non-linear gravitational clustering of dark matter, in which the effects of short-scale physics are encoded in an effective stress tensor and a set of counterterms. In this work, we develop a framework to investigate the physical origin of these counterterms by relating the effective stress tensor to the dynamics of individual dark matter halos in cosmological N-body simulations. We find that halo accretion contributes to the trace of the halo-based effective stress tensor, contributing to the background pressure and effective sound speed, while anisotropic accretion generates the traceless part, consequently contributing to the shear viscosity. For the halo population with $M_\mathrm{vir}>10^{12}\,M_\odot/h$, we find that halos account for $\sim\!95\%$, $\sim\!85\%$, and $\sim\!70\%$ of the full box background pressure, effective sound speed, and shear viscosity, respectively, depending on the EFT cutoff scale. The contributions to the counterterms are dominated by cluster-scale halos, $M_\mathrm{vir} \sim 10^{14} - 10^{15}\,M_\odot/h$ and halos with larger inflow rates, supporting an interpretation in terms of accretion-driven, out-of-equilibrium dynamics. Our results provide a short-scale, kinetic-theory interpretation of the EFTofLSS counterterms and establish a framework for connecting the properties and dynamics of short-scale structures to physics-informed priors for large-scale cosmological observables.
#### The connection between massive galaxies and their dark matter haloes from $z=0.25$ to $z \sim 4$ from the combined COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields
 - **Authors:** Natalia Stylianou, Matt J. Jarvis, Rohan G. Varadaraj, Peter W. Hatfield, Joel Hamlett, Catherine L. Hale, Tariq Yasin
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19030

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19030

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding how galaxies populate and grow within dark matter haloes is essential for linking the observed evolution of galaxies to the hierarchical growth of structure. We present a galaxy clustering analysis using optical and near-infrared data from the UltraVISTA and VIDEO surveys covering $9.3$ deg$^2$ across the COSMOS, XMM-LSS and ECDF-S fields, $\sim 5\times$ larger than previous comparable studies, providing sufficient volume to determine the halo occupation of the most massive galaxies to $z\sim 4$. We measure the angular two-point correlation function for stellar-mass-selected galaxies over $0.25<z<4$ and by combining the three fields, we reduce the cosmic variance by a factor of $\sim2$ compared to the individual fields. The stellar-to-halo mass ratio peaks at a halo mass of $M_{h}\approx10^{12} {\rm M}_{\odot}$ and is a maximum at $z\sim 1$. For the most massive galaxies, the peak halo mass ($M_{h}^{\rm peak}$) increases from $z=1\rightarrow 0$, while the peak stellar mass in such haloes remains approximately constant, indicating that at $z<1$ massive galaxy formation becomes less efficient. We also find a steady increase in the satellite fraction since $z\sim 1$, from which we infer that massive halos continue to grow hierarchically while their central galaxies experience suppressed stellar-mass growth. We suggest this is due to the central massive galaxies having their growth controlled by feedback from active galactic nuclei feedback, likely from low-power stochastic radio emission.
#### The Atacama Cosmology Telescope: DR6 Constraints on Isotropic Time-Dependent Cosmic Birefringence from Axion-like Particles
 - **Authors:** Zachary B. Huber, Huajian Yang, Steve K. Choi, Erminia Calabrese, Vera Gluscevic, Sigurd Naess, Michael D. Niemack, Lyman A. Page, Alexander van Engelen, Edward J. Wollack
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19049

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19049

 - **Abstract**
 Axion-like particles (ALPs) with sufficiently low masses are dark matter candidates because they could account for much or all of the observed dark matter energy density and help explain observations of galactic substructure. Such pseudoscalar fields could couple to photons, causing a time-dependent rotation of the polarization of the light with an oscillation frequency equal to the ALP mass. We use measurements of the polarization of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) by the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) between 2017 and 2022 to search for this effect using an EB power spectrum estimator. By probing oscillation periods from days to years, the data constrain the coupling between ALPs and photons for ALP masses between $2.5\times 10^{-23}$ eV and $4.8\times 10^{-20}$ eV. We find no evidence for such a signal and set a 95% upper bound on the axion-photon coupling constant, $g_{\phi\gamma} < 1.1\times10^{-11}\left({m_\phi}/{10^{-21}\,\mathrm{eV}}\right)\,\mathrm{GeV}^{-1} .$ This limit assumes that ALPs make up all of the dark matter and that the ALP field amplitude is stochastically distributed, which leads to more conservative bounds than the deterministic-field assumption used in some previous CMB polarization analyses. Among searches for ALP-photon coupling using CMB polarization, we set the strongest bounds to date under the stochastic-field assumption. This work develops a methodology to analyze over 10,000 time-sliced "depth-1" polarization maps from ACT, a data product that will be standard for time-domain astrophysics with the Simons Observatory, enabling searches with a higher sensitivity and observing span.

#### The Contribution of Mergers and Accretion to Dark Matter Halo Assembly: An Excursion Set Theory Approach
 - **Authors:** Hamed Kameli
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19312

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19312

 - **Abstract**
 We study the relative contributions of mergers and accretion to the assembly of dark matter haloes within the framework of Excursion Set Theory (EST). We extend a variance-discretized merger-tree algorithm, which recursively reconstructs all branches of a complete merger tree and introduces a merger-ratio criterion for distinguishing mergers from accretion. The method is validated by comparing the resulting progenitor distributions with the analytical conditional first up-crossing distribution, showing excellent agreement over a wide range of halo masses and redshifts. We find that the accretion contribution depends strongly on the adopted merger-ratio threshold. For large merger ratios ($MR=0.1$--$0.25$), accretion accounts for up to $\sim70\%$ of the mass growth of massive haloes, whereas including all resolved mergers ($MR=10^{-6}$) reduces the accretion contribution to $\sim40\%$. These values are in good agreement with previous N-body simulation results. The accreted mass exhibits a nearly self-similar distribution in the mass-redshift plane, with the dominant contribution arising from progenitors in the range $10^{-3}M_0 \lesssim M \lesssim 10^{-1}M_0$ and predominantly at $0.5 \lesssim z \lesssim 2$. Our results demonstrate that EST-based merger trees provide an accurate and computationally efficient framework for investigating halo assembly and quantifying the relative roles of mergers and accretion in hierarchical structure formation.

 - **Abstract**
 Ultraviolet (UV) observations offer unique insights into transient and variable astrophysical phenomena, from the Sun and nearby stars to supernovae and star-forming galaxies. Here, we summarise and review the diagnostic power of the UV spectrum for probing the physical conditions, dynamics and energetics of these events. We trace the historical development of UV astronomy from early satellite missions to the current landscape shaped by the Hubble Space Telescope, the GALaxy Evolution eXplorer, the Neil Gehrels Swift Observatory and AstroSat/UVIT. Despite remarkable progress, UV transient astronomy remains limited by instrument availability and cadence. Forthcoming missions promise to transform this field by capturing the rapid, luminous phenomena that define the dynamic Universe.

 - **Abstract**
 The small observed radius of neutron star XTE J1814-338 (with mass of $1.21_{-0.05}^{+0.05}$ $M_{\odot}$ and radius of $7_{-0.4}^{+0.4}$ $\rm{km}$) poses a great challenge to the traditional neutron star model composed of normal nuclear matter. The dark matter admixed neutron star (DANS) model provides a clue to solve this challenge. By employing the self-interacting fermionic dark matter model, we investigate the effects of dark matter parameters including the particle mass, self-interaction strength, and mass fraction of dark matter on the properties of DANSs. #### Low-cost neural summaries for cosmological inference: an ensemble of convolutional neural networks trained on approximate simulations
 - **Authors:** Iñigo Sáez-Casares, Davide Bianchi, Matteo Calabrese, Luigi Guzzo
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19891

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19891

 - **Abstract**
 Machine learning can build near-optimal summary statistics for extracting cosmological information from the large-scale structure of the Universe. Training them, however, requires large numbers of costly simulations. We aim to build a neural summary trained on fast, approximate low-resolution simulations that remains informative when applied to higher-resolution ones, reducing the cost of the training set. We train an ensemble of convolutional neural networks with different architectures to compress the matter overdensity fields of low-resolution particle-mesh simulations. The outputs of a subset of networks are concatenated into a summary statistic, which we use to constrain $\Omega_{\rm m}$ and $\sigma_8$ from high-resolution particle-mesh simulations, each about a thousand times more costly than their low-resolution counterparts. Theoretical predictions are provided by a Gaussian process emulator built from the high-resolution simulations. We test three strategies for selecting the smallest informative subset of networks. The neural summary extracts information beyond the binned power spectrum. Using multiple networks is essential: a single network can be less informative than the power spectrum. A greedy search based on posterior volume provides the tightest constraints with the fewest networks. The neural summary also outperforms the power spectrum for dark matter haloes, at different number densities and redshifts, despite being trained on low-resolution matter fields at $z=0$. This work demonstrates that an ensemble of networks trained on approximate simulations yields a summary statistic that remains informative on higher-fidelity data, including dark matter haloes. #### Revealing the Spatial Distribution of Dust and Plasma Solitary Waves in Jupiter's Innermost Magnetosphere Using Juno Waves Observations
 - **Authors:** Wenyu Zhang, Hao Cao, William S. Kurth, Mitchell M. Shen, Darrelle Wilkinson, Donglai Ma, Martin Brennan, George Hospodarsky, Scott J. Bolton
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20541

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20541

 - **Abstract**
 Jupiter's faint ring system contains micron- and submicron-sized dust whose dynamics are influenced by the planet's rapidly rotating magnetic field. Optical observations have established the morphology of the main ring and halo, but tenuous dust close to Jupiter remains difficult to characterize. We analyze nearly a decade of in-situ Juno Waves electric-field measurements using a two-stage convolutional neural network to identify and classify dust impacts, solitary waves, and other plasma waves. These in-situ dust-impact detections reveal a radially evolving structure extending well inside the traditionally observed halo. Together, these in-situ measurements extend the remote-sensing picture of Jupiter's rings and reveal previously unresolved dust and plasma structures in its innermost magnetosphere.
