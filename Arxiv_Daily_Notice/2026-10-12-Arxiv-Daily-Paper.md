#### When dust growth takes over: the origin of dust in post-reionization galaxies
 - **Authors:** Omima Osman, Gabriella De Lucia, Andreas. L. Faisst, Lizhi Xie, Fabio Fontanot, Michaela Hirschmann
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12591

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12591

 - **Abstract**
 The post-reionization epoch ($z\sim4$-6) marks a critical phase in galaxy evolution, during which galaxies rapidly assemble their stellar mass, enrich their interstellar medium, and build up substantial dust reservoirs. The physical processes responsible for such rapid dust enrichment remain uncertain. We investigate the dust enrichment history of the ALPINE-CRISTAL-JWST (ACJ) sample using the semi-analytic galaxy formation model GAlaxy Evolution and Assembly (GAEA), which follows galaxy assembly, chemical enrichment, and dust evolution. We select model galaxies matching the observed stellar masses, star formation rates, and metallicities of the ACJ sample, compare their dust properties with observed scaling relations, and trace their enrichment histories and environments. The model reproduces the observed dust mass-stellar mass relation and the dust-to-stellar mass, dust-to-gas, and dust-to-metal ratios as functions of stellar mass and metallicity. Reproducing these constraints requires efficient dust growth in the interstellar medium; stellar dust production alone cannot account for the observed dust reservoirs, even under the most optimistic assumptions. The combination of stellar mass, star formation rate, and metallicity provides a good predictor of galaxy dust content. Our model suggests rapid dust build-up consistent with the previously noted rapid metal enrichment, with evidence for a transition around $z\sim8$-9 below which dust growth becomes the dominant dust formation channel. Finally, ACJ-like galaxies preferentially reside in locally overdense environments, suggesting that environment may have contributed to their accelerated evolution. Taken together, these results support rapid and coupled stellar, metal, and dust build-up in ACJ galaxies during the post-reionization era.
#### Predicted state-to-state collisional rate coefficients from neural networks
 - **Authors:** Lisán David Cabrera-González, Carlos Cárdenas, Otoniel Denis-Alpizar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Chemical Physics (physics.chem-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12743

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12743

 - **Abstract**
 Molecular spectra observed in the interstellar medium are commonly analyzed using non-local thermodynamic equilibrium models, which requires accurate state-to-state collisional rate coefficients. Close-coupling calculations become prohibitively expensive for transitions from highly excited rotational states, therefore available datasets are often truncated at low initial states and temperatures; and since each new system also requires an accurate ab initio potential energy surface, many detected molecules have no collisional data. We employ two machine learning strategies to predict rotational state-to-state rate coefficients of molecules included in the train set beyond the range of initial states and temperatures computed so far, and predicting those of a molecule absent from training and validation sets. Feed-forward neural networks are trained on state-to-state de-excitation rate coefficients of 24 linear molecules colliding with para-H2. Scheme 1 includes all systems in the train+validation set, with 70%, 80%, and 90% initial-state boundaries and a 90% temperature boundary. Scheme 2 is trained on 22 systems described only by transition and molecular properties, and predicts two excluded systems, CS and CF+, whose astrophysical impact is explored with non-LTE calculations. For the 90% boundary, Scheme 1 predicts 99.4% of the test transitions within a factor of 2, and no prediction deviates from the reference value by more than a factor of 4.7. This model was used to complete 18 datasets. Scheme 2 reproduces the rate coefficients of two excluded systems within a factor of 3 for more than 92% of their transitions. Both schemes provide near-instantaneous complements to close-coupling calculations: Scheme 1 extends the range of a molecule already included in the training set with high accuracy, while Scheme 2 gives a reasonable first estimate for a molecule that has not been studied.
#### The Redshift-Space Galaxy Power Spectrum at Two-Loop Order
 - **Authors:** Oliver H. E. Philcox
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); High Energy Physics - Experiment (hep-ex); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); High Energy Physics - Theory (hep-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13867

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13867

 - **Abstract**
 Contemporary large-scale structure analyses center around the galaxy power spectrum, modeled with one-loop perturbation theory. This description naturally breaks down at large wavenumbers, prohibiting the extraction of cosmological information from small-scale clustering. In this work, we present a complete model for the redshift-space galaxy power spectrum up to two-loop order, derived in the framework of the Effective Field Theory of Large Scale Structure. This accounts for a wide variety of effects, including fifth-order galaxy bias, redshift-space distortions, small-scale non-linearities, spatial non-locality, long-wavelength bulk flows, coordinate distortions, and stochasticity, involving a total of 49 free coefficients. Our model additionally encodes all deterministic contributions to the one-loop bispectrum and trispectrum (as well as the tree-level quadspectrum and pentaspectrum), which depend on many of the same parameters. We implement the model numerically, using the \textsc{vegas} algorithm to precompute the loop integrals; this allows the full likelihood to be evaluated in milliseconds. The approach is validated using power spectra measured from high-resolution simulations, considering both dark matter and realistic galaxy samples. The extension to two-loop order greatly increases the range of scales over which the power spectrum can be modeled, with $k_{\rm max}$ rising from $0.2h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ to $0.34h\,\mathrm{Mpc}^{-1}$ for a luminous red galaxy sample at $z=0.5$. Much of the additional information is absorbed by the numerous fifth-order biases and counterterms; for this reason, constraints on $\sigma_8$ are not significantly enhanced by the inclusion of two-loop terms. Future work will assess the full implications of the two-loop model for cosmological parameter inference, both alone and in combination with higher-order statistics.
